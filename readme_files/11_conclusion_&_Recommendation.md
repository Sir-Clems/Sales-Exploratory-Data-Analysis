# Conclusion and Recommendation

## Final Summary

This exploratory data analysis provided a comprehensive view of the company’s **sales performance, customer behaviour, product performance, revenue distribution, and market concentration**.

The analysis found that the **United States and Australia were the strongest markets**, recording the highest sales values and sales volumes. This indicates that these markets represent important areas for maintaining sales performance and supporting future growth.

`Product analysis` revealed a significant difference between **sales volume and revenue contribution**. Accessories recorded the highest quantity sold, while **Bikes generated the overwhelming majority of total revenue**. At the individual-product level, the **Mountain-200 product line consistently appeared among the highest revenue-generating products**. This highlights the importance of distinguishing between unit demand and financial contribution when evaluating product performance.

`Customer analysis` also revealed substantial variation in customer value. While the overall customer base was relatively broad, a smaller group of customers generated substantially higher revenue and/or demonstrated high purchase frequency. These customers represent potential targets for retention and loyalty initiatives.

`Revenue` was relatively balanced across **gender and marital-status groups**, indicating that sales were not heavily concentrated within these demographic categories. However, demographic balance should not be interpreted as evidence of similar purchasing behaviour across all customers.

The analysis also identified **missing or unspecified customer and product attributes and potential age outliers**. Addressing these data-quality issues would improve the reliability of future reporting, segmentation, and predictive modelling.

Overall, the EDA establishes a foundation for making more informed decisions around **market prioritisation, product strategy, customer retention, inventory planning, and future predictive analytics**.


## Actionable Business Recommendations

Based on the findings from the analysis, the following actions are recommended:

### 1. Prioritise High-Performing Markets
Maintain strong inventory availability and targeted marketing activities in the **United States and Australia**, which recorded the highest sales volumes and revenues.

At the same time, investigate the reasons for lower performance in other markets before increasing investment. Factors such as customer demand, product preferences, pricing, distribution, and marketing effectiveness should be evaluated.

### 2. Protect the Core Bikes Revenue Stream
Because Bikes contribute the overwhelming majority of revenue, the company should prioritise **inventory availability, stock monitoring, and targeted marketing** for this category.

High-performing products such as the **Mountain-200 line** should receive particular attention because of their strong revenue contribution.

However, the company should also monitor its dependence on Bikes to reduce potential exposure to a single dominant product category.

### 3. Increase the Commercial Value of Accessories and Clothing

Accessories generated the highest unit volume but substantially less revenue than Bikes. This creates an opportunity to increase their commercial value through **cross-selling, product bundling, promotions, and targeted offers**.

Clothing should also be evaluated to identify opportunities for improving product assortment, pricing, and customer engagement.

### 4. Strengthen High-Value Customer Retention
Customers with high revenue contribution and/or frequent purchases should be prioritised for **retention and loyalty initiatives**.

Potential approaches include personalised offers, loyalty programmes, targeted promotions, and cross-selling based on previous purchasing behaviour.

Customer revenue, order frequency, average order value, and recency should be considered together rather than using a single metric to define customer value.

### 5. Investigate Lower-Performing Markets
Markets with lower sales volumes should be investigated to understand the underlying performance gap.

Further analysis should examine **customer concentration, revenue per customer, order frequency, product preferences, and average order value** across markets. These insights can guide more targeted regional marketing and product strategies.

### 6. Improve Data Quality
Missing customer and product attributes should be addressed through improved data-entry standards and validation procedures.

Potential age outliers should also be reviewed to determine whether they represent genuine customer records or data-entry issues. Improving data quality will increase confidence in future **business intelligence, customer segmentation, and predictive models**.


## Next Steps for Modelling

The exploratory analysis provides a foundation for more advanced analytics and predictive modelling. Potential next steps include:

1. Sales forecasting: Develop time-series models to predict future sales and support inventory planning.

2. Customer segmentation: Group customers based on purchasing behaviour, demographics, and revenue contribution.

3. Customer lifetime value (CLV) modelling: Estimate the potential long-term value of customers to support retention and marketing decisions.

4. Product recommendation modelling: Identify products frequently purchased together to support cross-selling and personalized recommendations.

5. Demand forecasting: Predict product-level demand to improve inventory management and reduce stock shortages or overstocking.

These next steps would move the project beyond *descriptive and exploratory analysis* toward *predictive analytics*, enabling the company to make more proactive and data-driven business decisions.


## Final Perspective
This project demonstrates how SQL-based exploratory analysis can transform raw transactional data into **actionable business insights**. The analysis identifies where revenue is concentrated, which products and customers contribute most to performance, how sales vary across markets, and where data-quality improvements are required.

The next stage is to build on these findings with **predictive modelling and advanced analytics**, moving the organisation from understanding what has happened to anticipating **what is likely to happen next and what actions the business can take in response**.
